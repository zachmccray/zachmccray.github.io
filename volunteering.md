<h2 class="content-heading" id="rocky-mountain-wildlife-alliance" tabindex="-1">Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance</h2>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="getting-started" tabindex="-1">Getting Started</h3>

<p>
Before relocating to Colorado in the latter half of 2024, I wanted to ensure I could continue volunteering closely with animals. I had garnered a bit of experience with
  <a href="https://www.owl-online.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Operation Wildlife (OWL)</a> – a wildlife rehabilitation center in Kansas – but was only a volunteer for a brief period of time. As a new volunteer, I was also limited in what I could do leading me to mostly working with feeder rodents and performing housekeeping duties. Along with OWL, I had experience volunteering at local animal shelters, namely 
  <a href="https://waysidewaifs.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wayside Waifs</a> in Grandview, MO and 
  <a href="https://www.lawrencehumane.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Lawrence Humane Society</a> in Lawrence, KS. I gathered nearly 500 hours between the two, but I always had a desire to expand the scope of work when volunteering with animals.
</p>

<p>
I discovered
  <a href="https://www.rmwalliance.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance (RMWA)</a> – an organization specializing in wildlife rehabilitation – through some Google searching as part of a list of things I wanted to accomplish before I moved, and eventually contacted the organization with an interest in volunteering. I communicated with Cookie Steen, the Volunteer Coordinator at the time, and after completing a phone interview, she graciously expressed her excitement about having me join the team. In May 2024, I officially became a volunteer at Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance.
</p>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="joining-the-team" tabindex="-1">Joining the Team</h3>

<p>
I trained under Emily Davenport, CWR, RVT – a Licensed Wildlife Rehabilitator – who is also board-certified in Wildlife Rehabilitation by the <a href="https://theiwrc.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">International Wildlife Rehabilitation Council (IWRC)</a>{% include footnote.html id="iwrc" num="1" %}, making her one of just four in the state of Colorado. I was quickly impressed with the extent of Emily's knowledge and ability to lead volunteers, and it didn't take me long before I felt comfortable completing much of the essential tasks as part of wildlife rehabilitation. As I became more confident in my abilities, I continued (and am still continuing) to learn more new ways to provide better care.
</p>

<p>
As a volunteer at RMWA, I perform a wide range of tasks, including but not limited to drawing and administering medications and fluids, enriching patient habitats, preparing specifically formulated diets, performing behavioral assessments, sterilizing and sanitizing habitats, equipment, and administrative spaces, participating in training and developmental opportunities, leading community outreach and educational programs, releasing rehabilitated animals, intaking patients and communicating with the public, and logging patient care information in a patient-record system.
</p>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="why-is-rocky-mountain-wildlife-alliance-important" tabindex="-1">Why is Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance Important?</h3>

<p>
Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance (RMWA) is a Colorado-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to advancing wildlife conservation through compassionate animal care and accessible community education. Rooted in a mission to serve both wildlife and people, RMWA blends professional rehabilitation practices with inclusive outreach programs that empower individuals to better understand and protect native species. With a focus on animal wellbeing, every patient receives personalized care guided by evidence-based protocols and enrichment-focused recovery. At the same time, RMWA offers a wide range of educational experiences – from youth workshops to public presentations – designed to foster empathy, ecological awareness, and lifelong advocacy for wildlife. Through its growing network of volunteers, educators, and wildlife ambassadors, RMWA is helping to build a more informed and compassionate community across Colorado.
</p>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="first-year-recap" tabindex="-1">First Year Recap</h3>

<p>
In my first year as a volunteer with RMWA, I contributed around 150 hours, primarily carrying out the above tasks, with some variance for things like the odd grocery store run here and there. I am looking forward to new opportunities in 2025, especially considering the growth of the organization itself. Leading into 2025, we were able to complete our fundraising goal and raise nearly $50,000 to purchase an x-ray machine. On top of that, we held our first open house, and have many more events planned for the future. To further emphasize our growth, as of May 2025, we now have 7 Educational Ambassadors, more than double from when I joined in May 2024! These Educational Ambassadors include Terra, an Ornate Box Turtle, Mimic, a Bull Snake, Fen, a Tiger Salamander, Atosha, a Great Horned Owl, Kingston, a Red-tailed Hawk, Roux, a Turkey Vulture, and the above-mentioned Yarrow, a Saw-whet Owl.
</p>

<h2 class="content-heading" id="denver-zoo-conservation-alliance" tabindex="-1">Denver Zoo Conservation Alliance</h2>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="background" tabindex="-1">Background</h3>

<p>
Similarly to how I approached Rocky Mountain Wildlife Alliance, I wanted to ensure if I were to volunteer with animals, I was only contributing to organizations who provide the best care to the animals in which they oversee. <a href="https://denverzoo.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Denver Zoo Conservation Alliance (DZCA)</a> is an accedited zoo by the <a href="https://www.aza.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA)</a>{% include footnote.html id="aza" num="2" %}. Less than 10% of zoos are accredited by the AZA, and DZCA shows why with their commitment to the care and well-being of their animals, in addition to their educational efforts throughout the community and abroad. Becoming a volunteer at DZCA requires an intensive training period, known as Zoo U, where the selected pool of applicants learn about the role they will play in supporting the 2,500+ animals under DZCA's care.
</p>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="becoming-a-volunteer" tabindex="-1">Becoming a Volunteer</h3>

<p>
After completing my training, I officially became a member of Denver Zoo Conservation Alliance's Volunteer Team. To begin my journey, I was required to complete several "Visibility Scans", which are surveys to collect observability/behavioral data on most animals at the zoo. These reports give the zoo a better idea of how visible each animal is, along with whether or not they are active or not. The zoo uses this data to make informed decisions on whether a habitat is beneficial to both the animal and the guests.
</p>

<p>
At the conclusion of my introductory volunteer shifts, I continued to perform additional Visibility Scans - a good excuse to go to the zoo and stop at each enclosure. Outside of these, I find myself assisting out with DZCA events and being an animal handling assistant.
</p>

<h2 class="content-heading" id="denver-food-rescue" tabindex="-1">Denver Food Rescue</h2>

<h3 class="content-subheading" id="combatting-food-insecurity-and-waste" tabindex="-1">Combatting Food Insecurity and Waste</h3>

<p>
An estimated 120 billion pounds of food are wasted each year in the United States. That is around 328 million pounds a day, 227 thousand pounds a minute, or nearly 3800 pounds every single second. The majority of wasted food is nutritious, fresh food harvested through the labor of farming and distributed at the mercy of our planet. When this food is thrown away, it’s not only meals that are lost – it’s the water, energy, land, and human effort that went into growing it.
</p>

<p>
At the same time, millions of people in our communities struggle with food insecurity. Wasting food in the face of this crisis is both a social and environmental injustice. Food rescue bridges that gap by redirecting would-be wasted food away from landfills and into the hands of people who need it most. I am passionate about these issues because I care about our planet, our communities, and our resources. Volunteering with Denver Food Rescue gives me the opportunity to take action on values that matter deeply to me. For me, volunteering with DFR ties together threads of compassion, sustainability, and equity into something tangible, and challenges me to think critically about injustices people face.
</p>
