<h2 class="content-heading" id="day-1-saturday-capulin-volcano-pecos-santa-fe-new-mexico" tabindex="-1">Day 1 – Saturday – Capulin Volcano, Pecos, & Santa Fe, New Mexico</h2>

<p>
We left early Saturday morning for Santa Fe, New Mexico, with stops at Capulin and Pecos along the way. We grabbed lunch at The Skillet in Las Vegas, NM. We both thought it was just fine, especially considering we were traveling through a state known for its food. Capulin Volcano National Monument was pretty neat, although I wish there had been a bit more signage and information available. There was an NPS ranger in the bowl of the volcano, which was a nice surprise for such a sparsely populated place. We completed both the Boca Trail and the Crater Rim Trail while there. One led around the base of the volcano through volcanic rock, while the other followed the rim. Overall, we were happy we visited and had the opportunity to stretch our legs in addition to checking another national monument off our list. Following our visit, we continued down the infamous Route 66 en route to our next stop.
</p>

<p>
Pecos National Historical Park, although small, was fascinating as well. I knew that we were going to discover a lot of history in New Mexico, and our trip was off to a great start. We didn't spend long here, but it was a nice introduction to Pueblo culture. The loop is quite small but gives you the chance to see prehistoric archaeological ruins. The most notable features were the kivas, which visitors were allowed to descend into, and the Pecos Pueblo Mission Church, a large structure originally built hundreds of years ago.
</p>

<p>
After arriving in Santa Fe, we drove just outside the city for a hike before night fell and made our way up Picacho Peak. The hike was very straightforward, and the views from the top were beautiful. I definitely recommend the hike and understand why it's popular in the area. We encountered only a handful of other people on the hike and were alone at the summit.
</p>

<p>
After traversing Picacho Peak, we drove to our hotel, where we quickly learned that Santa Fe might as well be known as "The City That Sleeps." Even though it was only 8:45, there were few food options available. I had been mildly aware of this when planning the trip but did not expect it to have such a significant impact. This was initially a bit of a letdown, as we were excited about the food in Santa Fe, but we ended up at Second Street Brewery, and it did not disappoint. I tried their famous "Alien Burger," which is described as a "1/3 lb all natural Black Angus patty topped with an organic blue corn dusted relleno, chile-jack cheese, smoked bacon, Second St Stout queso, Hatch green chile." Needless to say, it was incredible. We ordered chips and dip as an appetizer, Meriahna ordered tacos, and we enjoyed it all. Despite how impressed we were by the food, the real star of our night was the live music. I was able to hear one of the best—if not the best—bass players I have ever encountered.
</p>

<p>
Second Street Brewery was our last stop of the night. Although we enjoyed the food and live music, the noise began to wear on us, and we were both ready to call it a night. We had originally planned to visit the Puye Cliff Dwellings the next day; however, I checked their website and discovered that they would be closed, with plans to reopen the following day. Luckily, I knew from previous research that the Puye Cliff Dwellings were prone to closures and tended to be, unfortunately, understaffed. I had planned two days in Santa Fe for this reason, so we were able to switch our schedules for Days 2 and 3. With that in mind, we went to bed planning to head to Bandelier National Monument the following day.
</p>

<h2 class="content-heading" id="day-2-sunday-bandelier-santa-fe-new-mexico" tabindex="-1">Day 2 – Sunday – Bandelier & Santa Fe, New Mexico</h2>

<p>
We had a late start that morning, which was perfectly fine considering we had awakened at 5 a.m. the previous day and had only Bandelier National Monument on our schedule for the day. I had mapped out a few options for breakfast and Meriahna decided on "The Pantry". Even though we got there around 9 A.M. on a Sunday, the wait was only around 15 minutes. I ordered a breakfast burrito with Christmas chile and it was absolutely incredible. As a huge fan of breakfast burritos, especially living in Denver, I was extremely pleased with my order. Meriahna chose pancakes with a side of eggs and was a fan as well. So far the food had reached, or possibly even exceeded, my already lofty expectations.
</p>

<p>
Our first stop in Bandelier National Monument was the Tsankawi Village Trail, a short trail along a mesa where you can view cavates and petroglpyhs from the Ancestral Pueblo village known as Tsankawi. We went clockwise as we believed that was the best method for exploring the various sites. The hike itself was nice, and we were so focused on seeing the petroglpyhs that we spent much of the first half rounding the mesa looking at different rocks and taking our time to try and find the markings. We had completed most of the loop and had yet to see any definitive drawings and were becoming worried that we were missing a lot of artifcats. I checked online and found people noting much of what is to offer is actually at the end of the loop, and with that in mind we continued forward until we found many petroglyphs amongst the cliffs and cavates. The petroglyphs were remarkable, both in quantity and quality. A lot of our time was spent looking and photographing these markings. One hike into Bandelier National Monument and I would have been happy with my visit, but there were still a couple more trails to go.
</p>

<p>
Next, we drove deeper into the monument and stopped at the Alcove House Trail. The trail takes you on a short walk through some nice foliage, but the Alcove House isn't until the very end of the straightaway. Climbing the structure consists of 4 wooden ladders of varying lengths, and at the top nestled into a cliffside is what is known as the Alcove House. The dwelling is situated about 140 feet above Frijoles Canyon and served as a home to an estimated 25 people. The alcove itself provides a nice view of the canyon as well. There is also a kiva in the alcove which was reconstructed by NPS. Instead of retracing out steps back on the designated Alcove House Trail, we detoured to the Pueblo Loop Trail, an offshoot that redirects along the cliff face and allows you to view petroglyphs on your way back to the Visitor Center. I never fail to be amazed by the petroglpyhs. There were quite a few to view and the short trip off the path was well worth it. After returning to the visitor center, we realized it was later in the day than anticipated. We decided to omit the Falls Trail, especially since we heard the water level was declining, and head to Santa Fe for a spot to eat.
</p>

<p>
We wanted to visit the Santa Fe Plaza, and even though we arrived just before most vendors and folks started packing up, the town square was everything I had hoped for: charming, welcoming, and vibrant with culture. The vendors were all incredibly nice, the options were unique, the space was beautiful, and there was a nice offering of restaurants for dinner. I had seen that "The Plaza Cafe" is a well-visited, and for the msot part well revered, restaurant so we snagged a table in the corner just before it started to get busy. I ordered something simple as I wasn't feeling 100% percent and got a chicken quesadilla with a side of green chile. Meriahna got the crispy avocado tacos and was more than satisfied. After our dinner, we stopped for some ice cream nearby, and we grabbed some drinks at "Matador". I still wasn't feeling the best, so we didn't stay long, but we continued our trend of being pleased with what Santa Fe has to offer.
</p>
