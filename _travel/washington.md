<h3> Washington, USA </h3>
<p>September 18th - September 24th (2024)</p>

<div class="volunteer-text" style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
<h5>Getting Started</h5>

<p>
I had always wanted to travel to the Pacific Northwest, but travelling West in any capacity was not something I did often growing up. I am also not exactly certain when I decided to bridge that gap, but soon enough, there I was, planning a trip to Seattle and Olympic National Park. When I was much younger, my mom meticulously planned trips, finding important attractions, searching for local cuisine, and pinpointing exactly the best time of the year to go and visit the few places we did vacation to growing up. At the time, I never understood the urge to consistently be moving from one thing to the next, as I was much more fond of the hotel bed and pool. After all, it was a vacation, wasn't it? Then, I viewed it as a time to rest, relax, and do nothing – a nice break from the life of an elementary schooler. As I got older, I began to realize the importance of truly experiencing places different than the ones you know.
</p>
<p>
Following months of careful planning, we began with an early morning flight out of Denver, Colorado. After landing with an appetite, we rushed to get our rental car knowing the chaos that was awaiting us. The plane ride was quicker than listed, and I felt more at ease knowing we were already ahead of schedule – albeit we were only a few hours into our trip. It may have just been one out of many, many things to come, but it was also arguably the most important. It was highly volatile, mostly out of our control, and had the important role of setting up our trip for success.
</p>
<p>
The rental car process continued to push us ahead of schedule. We arrived at the counter with our reservations in hand and were happy to see there was no one else ahead of us. Once they confirmed our reservations and the transaction was complete, we made our way to choose our rental car, noting the line of people that had accumulated behind us. We got in our Toyota Camry, and began our time in Seattle by heading to Geraldine's Counter for some breakfast. The server was kind, the meals came out quickly, and the food was delicious. Even though we were feeling a bit of tiredness setting in, we were looking forward to the day ahead of us.
</p>
<p>
 Following our much-needed breakfast, we took a drive through the city to the Washington Park Arboretum to give us an opportunity to stand up and walk around. The weather was very typical for Seattle this time of year, with overcast skies hanging above our head, and cool air filling the space around us. The Arboretum offered a nice collection of winding paths through stretches of trees. Given our quick start to the trip, we decided to make our way to the Kubota Garden, a choice we were very happy with considering its proximity to the Arboretum and the relatively small size of the garden itself. The garden in tandem with the Arboretum offered us a promising start to our journey with their beauty.
</p>

<p>
One thing we never fail to do when visiting a new city is to make a stop at whatever zoo is available, especially those which are AZA accredited. After our trip to the Japanese Garden, we headed over to the Woodland Park Zoo. The zoo had an obscure layout, and unfortunately, a good chunk of the animals were not visible. However, we still enjoyed our time there, and we were thoroughly impressed by knowledge of some of the staff members. Most importantly, we were also continuing to make impressive time.
</p>
<p>
One thing many people had said to visit when in Seattle are some of the manay breweries, and we ended up at Fremont Brewing's Urban Beer Garden, which ended up being a mistake, but not for the reason you may expect. Given our early flight and a late night the day prior, we had been nonstop moving between places: the airport, breakfast, the Aboretum, the Japanese Garden, the zoo, and now the brewery. With the sun shining over our heads and an opportunity to finally sit down, coupled with a handful of drinks, we were slowly lulled into a state of poor decision making. After our time at the brewery we planned to take a tour of Smith Tower, grab a bite at Shawn O'Donnell's Irish Pub, and head out to a slew of local bars nearby. With such a strong first half of the day we were over an hour ahead of schedule. We both decided to check-in to our AirBNB and lay down until it was time to make our way over to the tower for our tour.
</p>

<p>
That was the biggest mistake we could have made.
</p>

<p>
I awoke later with a jolt, instantly understanding the situation. I could tell it was dark outside, and I felt far, far too rested to be awaking from a one hour nap, which is what we had planned to do. We had slept for over 5 hours, meaning it was past 9 PM. We missed our opportunity to tour the Smith Tower and eat our planned dinner. Wanting to salvage our day, we decided to head out to walk around Pike Place Market at night, even though we would miss most of what makes the area special given the time. We stopped by Alibi Room for some pizza, Zig Zag Cafe for some cocktails, and, of course, the infamous gum wall. The pizza at Alibi Room was seriously some of the best pizza I have ever eaten, and the drinks at Zig Zag, although pricey, were quite delicious as well. At this point it was nearly midnight and Meriahna was incredibly tired, as was I. We decided to make our way back to our AirBNB for the night knowing we had a day of driving and hiking awaiting us.
</p>
